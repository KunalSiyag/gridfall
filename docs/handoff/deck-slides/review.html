Every finding had to be reproduced before it was fixed, or rejected with evidence.

The payoff of the review step, with four real examples. One: the carjack throw. The implementer reported that the thrown driver cleared the open car door. A reviewer measured it on the real skeleton for all 17 car models: for 15 of 17 the gap was well under the 0.75 m target, and for sedans and hatchbacks the new throw was worse than the old one. It took three review rounds; the shipped numbers are closest 0.073 m from the door mesh with a full fling, and a known edge case at 0.014 m when a neighbouring car cuts the fling. Say out loud that all of that is measured headless and nobody has watched the animation in pixels yet. Two: the claim that with the carjack flag off behaviour is identical. A reviewer found one ungated change that still moved off-screen bodies; we documented it rather than hide it. Three: a Load the sim rejected had already replaced the live city while first person kept drawing the old one; Load now validates before it changes anything. Four: a mayor’s answer that was still in flight when New City or Load replaced the city landed in the new city; those answers are now dropped. The rule that made this work: findings must be reproduced before they are fixed, and a fixer may reject a finding if it brings evidence.
