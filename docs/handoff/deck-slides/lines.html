A war story that shows why “it looks fine on my machine” is not enough for a browser build. The report from play was: the web version draws lines, and the higher the graphics setting, the stronger they get. The lines you see in the browser are horizontal rows on the ground. Three different causes hid behind one complaint, and we only found them one at a time. First, the horizontal rows: screen-space ambient occlusion on Godot’s Compatibility renderer misbehaves when the 3D scale is below native; the web tier forces 0.6. Measured as row energy in grey levels (rms) it went from 10 to 13 at Low, Medium and High to 0.7 to 1.6 with SSAO off. Be careful with the explanation: the trigger is proven, but why the engine draws the rows is not established, and the commit says so. Second, rain: the emitter had 800, 1,600 or 3,000 particles depending on the preset, which is the one that literally grows with the quality setting; the web tier is now capped at 600 and aerial views get none. Third, the diagonal stripes on roads at Medium from the air: shadow-map acne, fixed with a Compatibility-only aerial normal bias; the absolute numbers depend on the sky hour, so quote them with that condition. Pixel evidence for all three fixes was produced in headless Chrome on a real GPU; the desktop Forward+ look was not re-checked.
