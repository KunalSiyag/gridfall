About 2 minutes. This is the image-model story. First the character, in three pictures. One, the sketch: before any 3D work I asked GMI's image model, hy-image-v3.5-preview, for a concept sheet of the five mayors as armoured characters. That gave us a look and a colour for each mayor: gold, blue, violet, green and red. Two, the model: a script in Blender, running with no window, built a simple robot from that idea, plus the crisis landmark buildings, which were sketched the same way. This picture is a fresh Blender render of that robot, a stand-in that proved the pipeline. Three, the game: for the street view we wanted real people, so the robots were replaced by forty realistic bodies (Microsoft Rocketbox, free to use) with animations from Quaternius, a free animation pack. A nurse looks like a nurse and a builder wears a hard hat, and a resident always gets the same body, even after you save and reload. This last picture is the real player character in the game. Be plain about it: the image model gave us the sketches and the direction, Blender turned them into the first models, and the final people come from free character packs. Then the posters, in one breath. The same image model made 28 posters that the game shows on billboards, bus shelters and shop fronts; the game draws the words on top, so the text is crisp. A mayor's slogan comes from their latest public post and the Gazette poster follows the latest headline. With a GMI key on the desktop build, a mayor's campaign poster is printed live by the image model, at most once every three game days, when the district's mood shifts (about 20 seconds; the pre-made one stays up meanwhile). No key or offline: only the pre-made set. Live posters are listed as desktop only and untested in the browser build. If you have a real in-game screenshot of a billboard, add it here.
