About 1 minute. What GridFall takes from GMI Cloud, in three jobs. The brains: fourteen different chat models, all through one connection. Each mayor has its own model, and so do the five city helpers and the residents when you talk to them. The artist: GMI's image model, hy-image-v3.5-preview, did two jobs. Before the game existed it drew the concept sheets that the Blender models were built from. Second, the posters. The game ships with 28 pre-made posters, drawn once with the image model. It shows them on each mayor's campaign billboards in their own district, the Gazette front page at bus shelters, shop adverts named after the real businesses, 'coming soon' hoardings at construction sites and warning notices near incidents. The game draws the words on top (slogans, headlines, shop names), so the text is always crisp. What they show is dynamic: a mayor's slogan comes from their latest public post and the Gazette poster follows the latest headline. The picture can be live too: with a GMI key on the desktop build, a mayor's campaign poster is printed by the image model, at most once every three game days per mayor, when the district's mood shifts. It takes about 20 seconds; the pre-made poster stays up meanwhile and is swapped when the new one arrives. With no key, or offline, only the pre-made set is used, and the same city always shows the same posters. In the browser build expect the pre-made set: live printing is listed as desktop only and has not been tested in a browser. The picture on the right is the pre-made set; swap in a real in-game screenshot when you have one. The home: GMI AgentBox runs the web build as a small container. Players use their own GMI key in their browser, so the server never holds a key. Also on the GMI platform but not used here: video and audio models, dedicated endpoints and GPU compute.
